# MiniCredit — internal notes

Not published. See `_notes/README.md`.

## Consumer credit — same standard as Feniko

See `_notes/feniko.md` for the editorial rules. Same treatment, one difference
that matters:

**MiniCredit has no standing free-first-loan offer.** Several competitors do.
That means a new borrower here pays ~298% RRSO from the first transaction, and
the page says so explicitly and links to Feniko as the comparison. Listing a
lender is not endorsing it over a cheaper alternative.

## Verified

Loans 500–9,600 zl, single repayment, typically 30 days, decision in about
15 minutes. Representative example: **2,500 zl over 30 days = 300.34 zl total,
297.61% RRSO**, comprising 250 zl commission, 20.55 zl service fee and 29.79 zl
interest. Nothing further charged if repaid on time.

## The point the page makes that most reviews miss

The commission is **83% of the total cost** and is fixed rather than accruing
with time, so early repayment saves far less than borrowers expect. That is the
single most useful thing a reader can learn here and it is in both the warning
block and the FAQ.

Also flagged: 9,600 zl is a large sum to fall due in one payment after 30 days.
The wide range reads as a feature in the lender's marketing; the page treats it
as something to think harder about.

## Occasional 0 zl promotion

Marked `status: unverified` on purpose. It surfaces periodically, is conditional
on on-time repayment, and is not dependable. The page tells readers to price the
loan from the representative example instead.

## Compliance

Same as Feniko. **Re-check the representative example on every verification
pass** — lenders update these and a stale RRSO on a credit page is a compliance
exposure.
