# MiniCredit — Loan Costs, RRSO and Terms

MiniCredit is a Polish short-term consumer lender (*chwilówka*) at [minicredit.pl](/go/minicredit), lending between 500 and 9,600 zł with decisions in about 15 minutes.

## ⚠️ What this actually costs

This is not a discount page and a loan is not a saving. MiniCredit's own representative example:

| Borrow | Term | Total cost | RRSO |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **2,500 zł** | **30 days** | **300.34 zł** | **297.61%** |

That 300.34 zł breaks down as a **250 zł commission**, a **20.55 zł service fee** and **29.79 zł interest**. Repaying on time adds nothing further.

Read the shape of that, not just the total: **the commission is 83% of the cost**. It is a fixed charge for taking the loan at all, so it does not shrink much if you repay early — unlike interest, which accrues with time. Borrowing this way for a few days costs nearly the same as borrowing for the full month.

**Unlike some competitors, MiniCredit does not run a standing free-first-loan offer.** A "0 zł" promotion appears periodically, but it is conditional and not always available — so the representative example above, not a 0% headline, is what a first-time borrower should plan against.

If a repayment date is already approaching on another debt, adding credit at ~298% will make that worse. Poland has free statutory debt advice; it is a better first call.

## About MiniCredit

MiniCredit operates entirely online: choose an amount on the site, complete a short registration if it is your first loan, and receive a decision in around **15 minutes**. The product is a classic *chwilówka* — a single-repayment short-term loan, typically over **30 days**, rather than a facility amortised across months.

Its range is wider than much of the market, from **500 zł to 9,600 zł**, which makes it usable for larger sums than a typical payday product. That width deserves a caution rather than praise: the cost structure that is merely expensive on 500 zł becomes a substantial liability at 9,600 zł over the same 30-day single repayment. A larger loan on these terms is not a longer loan — the whole balance still falls due at once.

Assessed honestly: MiniCredit is a legitimate, functioning lender and the process does what it says. But with no dependable free-first-loan offer, a new borrower here pays roughly **298% RRSO from the first transaction**, where several competitors — including [Feniko](/stores/feniko) — offer a genuinely free first loan. If you have decided to use this category of credit at all, that difference is worth checking before choosing a lender.
