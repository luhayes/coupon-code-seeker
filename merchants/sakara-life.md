## About Sakara Life

Whitney Tingle and Danielle DuBoise, friends since childhood, founded Sakara Life in **2011**. The company sits at the far premium end of meal delivery: not a meal kit you cook, not a diet plan, but finished organic plant-based meals designed around a nutritional philosophy and delivered ready to eat.

You choose how many meals a day and how many days a week — up to three meals a day across two, three or five days — and the menu rotates. The food is genuinely good and the ingredient quality is visible, which is the honest reason people stay.

Positioning honestly, because the numbers deserve to be stated plainly: meals run roughly **$28–$35 each**, which puts the three-day programme near **$676 a month** and the five-day near **$1,396 a month**. That is a restaurant budget, not a grocery budget. Sakara is not competing with meal kits on value and does not pretend to; it is competing with eating out, and it makes sense for people who are currently spending that money on takeaway and want it to be organic and plant-based instead. For anyone weighing it against cooking at home, the gap is very large.

Because the price is what it is, the 20% subscription differential is not a minor optimisation — on a five-day programme it is a difference of hundreds of dollars a month.

## How to order from Sakara Life

1. Choose your programme at [sakara.com](https://www.sakara.com) — how many meals a day, and how many days a week.
2. Select **subscription** rather than one-time. This is the 20% decision, and it is easy to miss because it is framed as a delivery preference rather than a discount.
3. Enter any promotional code in the discount field at checkout before paying.
4. Confirm the per-meal price in the order summary matches the subscription rate.
5. Deliveries can be paused or adjusted between weeks — pausing is usually better than cancelling and re-ordering at the one-time price.

**Stacking:** one promotional code per order. Because the subscription differential is built into the per-meal price rather than applied as a discount, a code generally applies on top of it — but confirm in the summary rather than assuming.
