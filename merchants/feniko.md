# Feniko — First Loan at 0% RRSO, and What It Costs After

Feniko is a Polish short-term consumer lender (*chwilówka*) operating at [feniko.pl](/go/feniko). Its first loan is genuinely free. Everything after it is among the most expensive credit available.

## ⚠️ Read the cost before the offer

This is not a discount page and a loan is not a saving. Two numbers matter:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **First loan** | **0% RRSO** — repay exactly what you borrowed, if repaid on time |
| **Every loan after** | **≈299% RRSO** |

A free first loan is a customer-acquisition offer. It is real and, used once and repaid on time, it costs nothing. The lender's business model, however, rests on the borrowers who come back — and the second loan is priced at roughly **299% annualised**.

**Borrow only if you know exactly where the repayment is coming from and when.** The failure mode with this product is not the first loan; it is refinancing it with a second, then a third, at full price. If you are considering this because a repayment date is already approaching on another debt, a short-term loan at 299% will make that worse, not better. Poland has free, statutory debt advice — talk to someone before adding a facility at this rate.

## About Feniko

Feniko lends online to Polish consumers, with an application process built for speed: a decision and transfer that can complete in about **15 minutes**. The headline product is the *chwilówka* — a small, short-term loan repaid in a single instalment rather than amortised over months.

Its acquisition offer is the standard one in this market: the **first loan free**, at **0% RRSO**, over **30 or 61 days**, with no commission, no interest and no hidden charges. Published caps on the free amount differ between sources — figures of 3,000 zł and 5,000 zł both circulate — and the general maximum across products is quoted at up to **7,000 zł**. Confirm the exact free amount on Feniko's own page, because the difference is material.

Assessed honestly: as a one-off, a genuinely 0% loan repaid on the due date is a legitimate way to bridge a short, known gap, and Feniko's is a real offer rather than a bait rate. The problem is structural to the category, not to this lender specifically. Repeat borrowing at ~299% RRSO turns a 5,000 zł loan into a debt that grows faster than most household budgets can absorb, and the speed of approval — a feature in the marketing — removes exactly the pause in which a borrower might reconsider.

### 1. The first loan is genuinely free

New customers repay exactly what they borrowed — **no commission, no interest, no hidden costs, 0% RRSO** — over 30 or 61 days. This is a real offer, not a teaser rate that converts. The condition is repaying **on time**; miss the date and the free status is what you lose first, alongside late-payment costs.

Check the maximum free amount on Feniko's own page before applying. Third-party sources quote 3,000 zł and 5,000 zł inconsistently, and we could not settle it.

### 2. Fast payout is a feature, not a discount

Funds can arrive in about **15 minutes**. Worth knowing if you have a genuine timing problem — and worth naming for what it is: speed does not change the price of the money, and in this category it mainly removes the interval in which someone might reconsider.

### 3. What repeat borrowing costs

After the free loan, commission and interest apply at roughly **299% RRSO**. That figure is listed here as a headline item rather than a footnote because it is the number that determines whether this product is a sensible tool or an expensive trap. At that rate, rolling a loan forward repeatedly is the single most costly thing a borrower can do.

## Before you apply

1. Write down the **exact date and source** of your repayment. If you cannot name both, do not borrow.
2. Confirm on [feniko.pl](/go/feniko) how much you can actually take at 0% — sources disagree.
3. Set a reminder several days before the due date. The whole value of the offer is conditional on repaying on time.
4. Decide in advance that you will **not refinance this with a second loan**. That is the decision that determines the real cost.
5. If money is tight enough that this is the option, use Poland's free statutory debt advice first. It costs nothing and it is a better first call than a 299% facility.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Feniko's first loan really free?**
Yes, if repaid on time. At 0% RRSO you repay exactly what you borrowed, with no commission or interest. Missing the repayment date is what removes that.

**What does a Feniko loan cost after the first one?**
Approximately **299% RRSO**. That is the number to plan around, not the 0% headline.

**How much can I borrow at 0%?**
Sources quote both 3,000 zł and 5,000 zł, and we could not confirm which is current. Check Feniko's own page before applying.

**How fast is the money paid out?**
As little as 15 minutes.

**Should I take a chwilówka?**
Only for a short, known gap you have a dated plan to close, and ideally only the free first one. At ~299% RRSO, repeat or rolled-over borrowing compounds very quickly. If the repayment plan is "another loan", that is the point to stop and get free debt advice instead.
